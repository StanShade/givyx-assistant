# Picking a car repair shop without getting burned

My neighbour Tom paid 900 dollars last spring for a brake job that should have cost half that. He didn't shop around. He just drove to the nearest place with a sign. That story is why I wrote this.

## Start with people you already know

Ask three people. Your co-worker, the guy who runs the corner shop, whoever. Most folks have one mechanic they swear by and one they warn you about. Both answers help.

If nobody has a name, check the reviews, but read the one-star ones first. A shop with fifty glowing reviews and two angry ones about "the bill doubled after they started" tells you more than the fifty.

## Go in with a small job first

Don't hand a new shop your engine. Book an oil change or a tyre swap. Watch how they treat you when the ticket is worth 60 dollars. Do they call before adding anything? Do they hand back the old parts if you ask? Does the invoice match the quote?

I did this with a place on Fourth Street two years ago. They wrote "brake pads at 3 mm, will need doing by autumn" on the receipt and didn't push me to do it that day. I went back for the pads. Still go there.

## Get the quote in writing

A verbal "should be about two hundred" means nothing once the car is on the lift. Ask for parts and labour on paper, or at least in a text message. If they won't do that, walk. Plenty of shops will.

Two quotes are enough. Three if the numbers are far apart. You don't need five.

## Questions that sort the good from the bad

- "Can I see the old part?" A straight answer is a good sign.
- "Is that price with parts?" Some quotes quietly leave them out.
- "What happens if it takes longer?" You want to hear "we call you", nothing else.
- "Who does the work?" Sometimes it's the owner. Sometimes it's a kid who started last week.

## What I'd skip

Skip any shop that finds four new problems every visit. Skip the one that can't say when the car will be ready. And skip the one that gets annoyed when you ask why. Annoyed mechanics make expensive mistakes, and you pay for them.

## The short version

Ask around. Test them with something cheap. Get it on paper. Then stick with the one who treats a 60-dollar job the same way they treat a 900-dollar one. That's it. Tom found a new shop this way and he's been happier since, and so has his wallet.
